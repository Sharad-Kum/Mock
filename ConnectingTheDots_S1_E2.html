Connecting the Dots · Series 1 · Edition 2 · 9 Sep 2026

Integration is not an interchange — Absence is not a fracture.

GTM, Sales and Marketing are increasingly discussed as one outcome: Growth.

Some organizations are bringing them under a single growth leader; others keeping separate peer leadership layers. One size does not fit all is so very true.

Chief Growth Officer hiring has climbed 117% since 2019 — one of the fastest-rising C-suite titles. The demand for a unified leader to own growth end-to-end is real. So is the risk of the title outrunning the mandate.

Organizations that have integrated the function have been conscious about integration not becoming an interchange. Each function works through a distinct lens, and any leader holding more than one must switch hats deliberately — without letting one influence the others.

Picture that same leader deciding a client's renewal terms on Monday, then standing at a product launch on Thursday. The moment the renewal math starts shaping how the launch story gets told — or the launch narrative starts softening what the renewal actually needs — integration can quietly become interchange.

Three structures, one discipline
Xero folded Sales, Marketing, Business Development and Customer Experience under Maninder Sawhney as Chief Business Officer — the stated goal: faster growth, sharper customer focus. Weeks in, still early.
ghd dissolved its Chief Marketing & Digital Officer role for a single Chief Growth Officer, Liz Griffiths — in the CEO's own words, meant to create ‘greater consistency, agility and impact’ across markets. Same story — early days.
N-able named Russell Rosa Chief Revenue Officer to unify sales, channel and partner functions under one mandate for upmarket growth. 5 weeks later, ARR of $544.5M, up 6.0% year-over-year, and a 28.9% EBITDA margin were disclosed.

Different structures, one shared discipline. None of them are proof yet — intent is visible.

Structures change. Functions integrate. Accountability cannot dilute — because when one dimension dilutes, the outcome dilutes, and everything revolving around it gets diluted

That's the line between growth architecture and growth theatre. One holds every dimension accountable. The other borrows credibility from proximity.
